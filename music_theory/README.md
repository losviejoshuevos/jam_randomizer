# Music theory references

This directory contains local reference material used to design and review the
Jam Randomizer harmony engine.

## The Berklee Book of Jazz Harmony

Local file: `Joe Mulholland & Tom Hojnacki - The Berklee Book of Jazz Harmony (Berklee Guide) [2013].pdf`

The complete 264-page scan was reviewed on 2026-08-05. The review covered the
introduction, all 11 chapters, both appendices, musical examples, lead sheets,
author notes, and index.

### Generator-relevant principles

- Treat tonic, subdominant, and dominant as musical functions, not merely as
  scale-degree labels.
- Judge a progression together with harmonic rhythm and metrical placement:
  stable harmony generally belongs on stronger accents, while unstable harmony
  can create forward motion on weaker accents.
- Prefer short, recognizable functional units for an accessible jam card:
  tonic prolongation, IV or ii departure, dominant preparation, and return.
- Use root-motion families deliberately. Descending fifths create the strongest
  forward drive; stepwise and third-based movement are useful for contrast and
  prolongation.
- Let the harmony-freedom setting control chromatic color progressively:
  diatonic chords first, then secondary dominants, parallel-minor borrowing,
  diminished approaches, tritone substitutions, modulation, and finally
  non-functional constant structures.
- Borrowing from the parallel minor is especially useful for an understandable
  B section: iv, bVI, bVII, and bII colors can darken a major-key progression
  without requiring a full key change.
- Preserve context when using dominant-quality chords. In blues and modal
  music, a dominant-seventh chord does not necessarily have dominant function.
- Blues should retain its functional 12-bar map even when reharmonized. Its
  melodic language is not adequately described by one universal “blues scale.”
- Modal material depends on repetition, slow harmonic rhythm, characteristic
  scale tones, and avoidance of cadences that accidentally establish the
  relative major.
- Advanced chord extensions and voicings are optional presentation layers. The
  MVP card should keep chord symbols readable for jamming and avoid requiring
  musicians to understand academic functional notation.

### Scope note

The book is a jazz-harmony reference, not a funk-style manual. It is most useful
for validating harmonic function, tension, borrowing, cadence, voice leading,
and controlled chromaticism. Funk-specific vocabulary must still be derived
from suitable repertoire and style references.

The commercial PDF should remain a local reference unless its redistribution
rights have been confirmed before committing it to a public repository.
