# Neo-Soul / Modern R&B style research

## Scope

This is a compositional study, not a chart ranking. Twenty contrasting recordings were selected to model jam-friendly neo-soul, quiet storm, 1990s R&B, hip-hop soul, gospel R&B and contemporary R&B. Full copyrighted forms are not reproduced; observations are transformed into original harmonic rules.

## Twenty reference recordings

| # | Recording | Direction | Form / harmonic observation | Generator lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D’Angelo — “Brown Sugar” | Foundational neo-soul | Repeating groove, blues/jazz chord color and vocal development over a stable pocket. | Let one compact loop carry A; development may be rhythmic rather than harmonic. |
| 2 | D’Angelo — “Lady” | Neo-soul / funk | Short syncopated harmonic cell, rich seventh/ninth color and strong bass identity. | Two-state vamps should be common and extensions must preserve the root function. |
| 3 | D’Angelo — “Untitled (How Does It Feel)” | Slow neo-soul | Spacious slow build with gospel-inflected harmony and increasing arrangement intensity. | Long chord durations are valid; B can lift through predominant/dominant motion. |
| 4 | Erykah Badu — “On & On” | Hip-hop soul | Minimal minor-centered loop with melodic phrasing creating most of the movement. | A one- or two-chord minor theme must remain available at every complexity. |
| 5 | Erykah Badu — “Other Side of the Game” | Neo-soul vamp | Extended groove, ambiguous functional pull and warm minor-seventh color. | Avoid forcing a cadence into every loop; use color and duration. |
| 6 | Erykah Badu — “Didn't Cha Know” | Sample-based neo-soul | Circular harmony and loose microtiming create a hypnotic, non-teleological form. | Hip-hop-soul archetype favors two/four-chord cycles and weak cadential pressure. |
| 7 | Maxwell — “Ascension (Don't Ever Wonder)” | Up-tempo neo-soul | Danceable major/minor-seventh loop with a clear chorus lift. | A can groove on a compact loop; B changes register/function without harmonic overload. |
| 8 | Maxwell — “Whenever Wherever Whatever” | Quiet storm | Slow, spacious guitar-led harmony with expressive inner motion. | Quiet-storm patterns use longer spans and fewer chord events. |
| 9 | Lauryn Hill — “Ex-Factor” | Hip-hop soul ballad | Minor-centered verse with a stronger gospel/soul release and layered outro. | Theme B should provide emotional lift without abandoning the tonal identity. |
| 10 | Lauryn Hill & D’Angelo — “Nothing Even Matters” | Neo-soul duet | Warm extended harmony, long phrases and relaxed functional movement. | Prefer maj7/maj9, m7/m9 and stepwise guide-tone connections. |
| 11 | Jill Scott — “A Long Walk” | Neo-soul groove | Repetitive live-band pocket with jazz-colored chords and conversational phrasing. | Generate playable loops rather than dense jazz standards. |
| 12 | Jill Scott — “He Loves Me (Lyzel in E Flat)” | Gospel neo-soul | Repeated harmonic field grows through vocal and ensemble dynamics. | Repetition is intentional; section development may simplify or intensify the same idea. |
| 13 | Groove Theory — “Tell Me” | 1990s R&B | Compact diatonic/minor loop, clear hook and restrained harmonic rhythm. | 1990s R&B should remain accessible at easy and medium levels. |
| 14 | Tony! Toni! Toné! — “Anniversary” | R&B / quiet storm | Long-form slow groove with soul/jazz extensions and clear phrase cadences. | Rich symbols belong at slower harmonic rhythm. |
| 15 | Aaliyah — “One in a Million” | Contemporary 1990s R&B | Sparse minor harmony, syncopated production and repetition define the track. | Modern R&B can be harmonically minimal; do not confuse complexity with chord count. |
| 16 | Floetry — “Say Yes” | Neo-soul ballad | Cyclic extended-chord progression supports spoken and sung improvisation. | Favor loopability and common-tone voice leading. |
| 17 | Musiq Soulchild — “Just Friends (Sunny)” | Up-tempo neo-soul | Bright groove, jazz-derived chord colors and compact repeated sections. | Medium tempo can use more frequent changes while remaining cyclical. |
| 18 | Anthony Hamilton — “Charlene” | Southern / gospel R&B | Traditional soul cadence enriched by gospel approach chords and vocal call-response. | Gospel R&B gets selective secondary dominants/backdoor motion, not constant reharmonization. |
| 19 | Daniel Caesar feat. H.E.R. — “Best Part” | Contemporary neo-soul | Four-chord acoustic loop with seventh colors and very slow harmonic rhythm. | A simple four-chord cycle can sound modern through extensions and space. |

## Resulting internal archetypes

1. `neo-soul-vamp`: one-to-four chord loops, slow harmonic rhythm, rich but coherent color.
2. `nineties-rnb`: clear cyclic harmony, accessible hooks and restrained extensions.
3. `quiet-storm`: slow tempo, long durations and expressive predominant/dominant lift.
4. `hip-hop-soul`: minimal minor loops and weak cadential pressure.
5. `gospel-rnb`: call-response form, minor subdominant, secondary dominant and backdoor color.
6. `contemporary-rnb`: pop-derived cycles with modern seventh/ninth voicings and sparse chromaticism.

## Generator constraints

- Harmonic freedom changes roots and borrowed/secondary functions; complexity changes extensions.
- Easy uses readable triads; medium widens to 6/7/9/sus; advanced adds 11, 13sus, maj7#11 and rare altered dominants.
- Advanced retains plain and seventh chords instead of maximizing every suffix.
- A is loopable and memorable. B/C contrast through function, center of gravity or harmonic rhythm.
- One high-impact chromatic device is preferable to several unrelated substitutions.
- Neo-soul should not become bebop: ii–V chains are selective, loops and common tones dominate.
