# Country / Americana style research

## Scope

The twenty recordings cover honky-tonk, Bakersfield, outlaw country, folk/Americana, country rock, ballad and waltz traditions. The generator translates 2/4/train-beat ideas into the current 4/4/3/4 MVP without attempting to encode instrumental licks.

## Resulting internal archetypes

1. `honky-tonk`: I/IV/V, secondary-dominant walkups and direct cadences.
2. `bakersfield`: brighter/faster I/IV/V frames and concise changes.
3. `outlaw-country`: modal ♭VII color, rock influence and relaxed loops.
4. `americana-folk`: familiar diatonic four-chord cycles and narrative space.
5. `southern-country-rock`: I–♭VII–IV and minor modal frames.
6. `country-ballad`: long durations, vocal space and restrained harmonic lift.
7. `country-waltz`: 3/4-oriented I/IV/V language without half-bar events.

## Generator constraints

- Plain triads remain frequent even at advanced complexity.
- Dominant 7, sus2/sus4, add9 and 6 are preferred over dense jazz tensions.
- ♭VII, II7 and VI7 are controlled color; remote chromatic roots remain rare.
- Fast honky-tonk/Bakersfield patterns use fewer harmonic events.
- Country waltz strongly prefers 3/4; other archetypes strongly prefer 4/4.
- Current MVP represents train-beat/2/4 behavior inside 4/4 until 2/4 is added.
