# Funk harmony research for the MVP generator

## Scope

This is a working top ten of widely recognized, style-defining Funk recordings
by different artists. It is not presented as an objective popularity chart.
The selection cross-checks songs and artists repeatedly represented in
Billboard-derived lists, the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame's influential-song list,
and genre histories.

Only the principal vamp or riff harmony is used. Full song forms, melodies,
lyrics, signature rhythms, and literal transcriptions are deliberately excluded
from the generator.

## Reference recordings and harmonic observations

| # | Recording | Main-vamp observation | Generator lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | James Brown — “Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine” | A very long Eb9/Eb13 groove; the bridge changes to a second groove. | A card may stay on one dominant root and change only its upper color, such as 9 to 13. |
| 2 | Sly & the Family Stone — “Thank You (Falettinme Be Mice Elf Agin)” | Essentially one Em/Em7 harmony; E–D–B motion belongs primarily to the bass riff rather than three chord functions. | Do not turn every bass note into a chord. One minor chord can be the complete section. |
| 3 | Stevie Wonder — “Superstition” | The verse is a static Eb-minor/pentatonic center. A short contrasting passage uses altered and chromatically adjacent dominant colors. | Prefer a static minor vamp; permit short chromatic dominant side-slips as a rarer color. |
| 4 | Parliament — “Flash Light” | A stable minor center supports a chromatic, highly melodic synth-bass line. | Riff motion does not require functional T–S–D changes; keep the harmony static or mode-ambiguous. |
| 5 | Commodores — “Brick House” | The verse alternates D/A and Am7: a compact A-Dorian tonic/major-IV relationship over a strong center. | Add two-chord Dorian `i–IV` vamps, including repetitions that can finish on IV. |
| 6 | Kool & the Gang — “Jungle Boogie” | The main identity comes from a repeated G-centered riff, not a resolving progression. | Weight single-chord and riff-centered cards above functional turnarounds. |
| 7 | Rufus feat. Chaka Khan — “Tell Me Something Good” | Dorian planing and descending chord shapes lead to a Gb9/Gb13 color vamp. | Allow modal planing and same-root 9/13 color changes without requiring a cadence. |
| 8 | Earth, Wind & Fire — “Shining Star” | The verse sits on E9; the contrasting hook moves dominant shapes A7–D9–G7–C9. | Add rare non-functional dominant-planing patterns alongside the much more common one-chord vamp. |
| 9 | Ohio Players — “Fire” | A riff-centered F-minor field is set up with a C9 horn/guitar color. | Permit a minor tonic field with a dominant-color stab, without forcing classical resolution. |
| 10 | Wild Cherry — “Play That Funky Music” | The main E9 vamp uses F#9/F9 side-slips; a short C7–B7–Em turnaround appears as contrast. | Add whole-step/half-step dominant side-slips and keep cadential turnarounds rare. |

## Rules encoded in the generator

1. Single-chord vamps remain the most strongly weighted family.
2. Long harmonic rhythm (four or eight bars per chord) is normal, not a fallback.
3. Medium major cards can use a dominant-tonic `I9`; advanced cards use `I13`.
4. Medium and advanced minor cards can use Dorian `i7–IV9` and `i11–IV13`.
5. Adventurous major cards may use `I–II–bII–I` side-slip shapes with 9ths or 13ths.
6. Advanced same-root motion can alternate `I13` and `I13sus4`.
7. Dominant-shape planing is available at low weight.
8. Functional T–S–D turnarounds remain available but deliberately uncommon.
9. Open endings on IV or a color chord remain valid.
10. Complexity boundaries remain strict: Easy uses only triads, Medium uses 7ths/9ths, Advanced uses upper or altered extensions.
