# Reggae / Dub style research

## Scope

The twenty recordings cover ska/rocksteady roots, roots reggae, one-drop, dub, lovers rock, dancehall and reggae rock. The generator models harmony and section contrast; authentic rhythm-section instructions remain a later layer.

## Resulting internal archetypes

1. `roots-reggae`: three/four-chord diatonic and modal cycles.
2. `one-drop`: one/two-chord spacious themes and restrained releases.
3. `rocksteady`: moderate tempo, doo-wop-like cycles and clear vocal form.
4. `dub-vamp`: one/two chords, maximum space and arrangement-led development.
5. `reggae-rock`: pop/rock harmonic cycles with reggae pacing.
6. `ska`: faster tempo and more frequent I/IV/V or turnaround motion.

## Generator constraints

- One- and two-chord results are intentionally common.
- Extensions are much sparser than in Soul/Jazz; advanced widens the palette but keeps plain chords.
- Dub avoids routine dominant resolution and dense bridges.
- Ska has the fastest BPM range and the most frequent changes.
- Reggae identity ultimately depends on drum, bass and offbeat-comping prompts; harmony alone must stay simple enough to accept those parts.
